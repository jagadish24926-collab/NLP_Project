Albert Einstein was born on 14 March 1879 in Ulm, Germany.
He is widely regarded as one of the most influential physicists of the twentieth century.
In 1905, often called his miracle year, Einstein published four groundbreaking papers.
One of these papers introduced the special theory of relativity.
Another paper explained the photoelectric effect, for which he received the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1921.
In 1915, Einstein completed his general theory of relativity.
The general theory of relativity describes gravity as the curvature of spacetime.
In 1919, Arthur Eddington confirmed one of Einstein's predictions during a solar eclipse.
Einstein emigrated to the United States in 1933 after the rise of the Nazi party.
He joined the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton, New Jersey.
Einstein became an American citizen in 1940.
He is famous for the equation E equals mc squared, which expresses the equivalence of mass and energy.
Einstein died on 18 April 1955 in Princeton.
His brain was preserved for scientific study after his death.
Today, Einstein's name is synonymous with genius around the world.
The Nobel Prize is awarded annually in Stockholm, Sweden.
Marie Curie won the Nobel Prize in Physics in 1903 and in Chemistry in 1911.
Isaac Newton formulated the laws of motion and universal gravitation in the seventeenth century.
